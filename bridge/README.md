# Bridge positions: Rhine-Ruhr and the Frankfurt corridor

Goal: a paid position reachable from Cologne or Düsseldorf, to bridge the time
until a PhD start. Target pay above 1,200 euros a month, and in practice much more
than that is achievable (see below).

_Last updated: September 2026._

## The geography

In range from Cologne or Düsseldorf, roughly an hour or less: **Bonn** (30 min),
**Essen** and **Duisburg** (25 to 40 min from Düsseldorf), **Leverkusen**,
**Wuppertal**, **Bochum**, **Dortmund**, **Aachen**.

**Frankfurt is in range too**, via the Cologne to Rhine/Main high-speed line.
Köln Hbf to Frankfurt Flughafen Fernbahnhof is about an hour, and the Sprinter
services are faster. Two practical qualifications, neither of them fatal.

First, the offices are not at the airport. Add roughly 30 to 40 minutes of S-Bahn
and U-Bahn to reach the ECB in Ostend, the Bundesbank in Ginnheim, KfW in the
Westend, or the Goethe campus. Realistic door to door is about 1h30 to 1h45 each
way, so this is a two or three day office week with hybrid arrangements, not a
daily commute.

Second, the ICE is not covered by the Deutschlandticket. A BahnCard 100 runs
around 4,600 euros a year in second class. That is trivial against an ECB or KfW
salary and it is most of an internship stipend, which is why the pay threshold
below matters more for Frankfurt than for anywhere else. Check every Frankfurt
posting for two things, the number of required office days and whether there is a
travel allowance or Jobticket.

Also in the Frankfurt orbit: **Wiesbaden** and **Mainz**, about 1h30, which
matters because Destatis is there.

Still out. **Luxembourg** at over three hours, and **Brussels** at about 1h50 from
Cologne. Brussels is closer than people assume, but 1h50 each way is a relocation
question rather than a commute, so the EIB and the Brussels institutions come back
only if you would move.

## The bigger shift: stop applying for internships

Once the M.Sc. is finished in September 2026 you are a graduate, which makes you
eligible for **wissenschaftliche Mitarbeiter** posts (TV-L E13) at German
universities and institutes, and for graduate-entry schemes at the central banks.
These are salaried jobs, not internships.

The difference is large. A 65% E13 post pays roughly 2,700 to 3,000 euros gross a
month, against 1,200 to 2,200 for an internship. Many of these posts are also
formally **Doktorandenstellen**, meaning the job and the PhD are the same
position. That solves both of your problems at once and is a far better use of the
same application effort.

The clearest illustration is the ECB. The traineeship pays about 1,170 euros a
month, which would not survive the commute. The **ECB Graduate Programme** is a
two-year contract for recent postgraduates paying around 4,900 euros a month. Same
employer, same building, roughly four times the money, and finishing the M.Sc.
makes you eligible.

So the priority order is: graduate schemes and E13 research posts first,
internships only as a fallback.

## One timing trap worth naming

The bridge window is roughly October 2026 to September 2027, because the German
winter cycle starts PhDs in October 2027. The fixed-cohort graduate schemes do not
actually fill that window. The ECB Graduate Programme runs on annual cohorts
starting each September, and the 2026 cohort has already begun, so the next one
starts in September 2027, which is when a PhD would start anyway.

That does not make it a bad target. It makes it a **parallel bet** rather than a
bridge, and a good hedge if the PhD round does not land. Apply to it on that
basis. The 2026 cohort was advertised around February 2026, so watch
talent.ecb.europa.eu from January.

What genuinely fills the gap is the rolling-start work: E13 posts at chairs and
institutes, Bundesbank research assistant contracts, BaFin, Destatis, GIZ. Those
are advertised throughout the year and can start within weeks. Put the volume
there.

## In-range targets

### Frankfurt and Rhine-Main, about an hour by ICE

- **European Central Bank, Graduate Programme.** Two-year fixed-term for recent
  postgraduates, around 4,900 euros a month, two rotations across business areas.
  The single best-paid target on this list. Requires EU nationality, the same test
  you already cleared to register for EPSO AD5. There is also a separate
  **Economist Graduate Programme** and the research traineeships in DG Research.
- **Deutsche Bundesbank, Forschungszentrum.** Research assistant posts at E13,
  recently advertised at roughly 32,900 to 47,500 euros depending on experience,
  often as a two-year 50% contract, so check the FTE before comparing. The work is
  empirical microeconomics on survey and firm-level data. Not a perfect field
  match, but the method is yours.
- **KfW**, including KfW Research and DEG. Graduate entry and internships.
- **BaFin.** Dual seat in Bonn and Frankfurt, so the Bonn office is 30 minutes
  away rather than 90. Check both locations on every posting.
- **Destatis, Statistisches Bundesamt, Wiesbaden.** Worth a separate look because
  it hosts the **Forschungsdatenzentrum** of the statistical offices, which is the
  German equivalent of the administrative data infrastructure your thesis ran on.
- **Goethe University Frankfurt** chairs and **SAFE**, the Leibniz Institute for
  Financial Research, for E13 posts. **Frankfurt School of Finance and
  Management**.
- **Lufthansa Group** at the airport itself, which is the shortest commute on this
  entire list, plus **Deutsche Börse**, the large banks, and the big four.

### Bonn, 30 minutes from Cologne

- **IZA, Institute of Labor Economics.** The best field match anywhere in range.
  Labour economics, administrative data, policy evaluation. Look for research
  assistant and junior research associate posts.
- **GIZ.** Roughly 2,200 a month for an internship, and also hires graduates.
- **briq, Behavior and Inequality Research Institute.**
- **Max Planck Institute for Research on Collective Goods.**
- **University of Bonn chairs and ECONtribute.** E13 posts, often with a PhD.
- **BaFin Bonn**, and the federal ministries with Bonn seats.
- **Deutsche Post DHL** and **Deutsche Telekom** head offices, for corporate
  economics and analytics.

### Cologne, home

- **University of Cologne chairs.** Siegloch, Pinger, and van Maarseveen are
  already contacts. Chair-level E13 posts are exactly the job-plus-PhD case.
- **ECONtribute**, the Bonn and Cologne cluster.
- **IW Köln**, Institut der deutschen Wirtschaft, an economic research institute.
- **Bayer** in Leverkusen, 20 minutes away.

### Essen and Duisburg, 25 to 40 minutes from Düsseldorf

- **RWI Leibniz Institute, Essen.** Doctoral researcher posts at TV-L E13.
  Bachmann has no slot at his chair, but other groups recruit, and Westphal and
  Tamm are the closer field fits.
- **University of Duisburg-Essen**, an RGS Econ partner.
- **E.ON** and **Evonik**, Essen.

### Düsseldorf, home

- **HHU and DICE.** The PhD programme was dropped, but E13 research posts are a
  separate thing and still worth watching.
- **Henkel**, **Metro**, **Vodafone**, **Trivago**, **Ergo**, and the big four
  offices.

### Bochum and Dortmund, 45 to 60 minutes

- **Ruhr-Universität Bochum** and **TU Dortmund**, both RGS Econ partners, so a
  post there can also be the PhD route.

## Qualification filter

Reject immediately if any of these is true.

- Unpaid, or below 1,200 euros a month
- Outside the range above, meaning further than Frankfurt or Dortmund
- In Frankfurt, below about 2,500 euros a month or requiring five office days,
  since the commute cost and time only make sense above that
- Requires current enrolment (Werkstudent, Pflichtpraktikum)
- Requires a completed PhD
