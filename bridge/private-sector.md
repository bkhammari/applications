# Finance, consulting and industry

The other track. Everything here is within the Cologne, Düsseldorf and Frankfurt
range set out in [`README.md`](README.md).

_Last updated: September 2026._

## Say the trade-off out loud first

Most of these are career jobs, not bridges. A strategy consultancy or a bank
hiring a graduate expects several years, and applying while intending to leave in
twelve months for a PhD is a real conflict rather than a technicality. It is worth
being clear with yourself about which of two things you are doing.

If the PhD is still the goal, the honest bridge options are economic consulting
and research-adjacent analytics, where a one or two year analyst stint before a
doctorate is normal and often expected.

If the PhD round does not land, a consulting or finance job is a far better
outcome than a poorly paid internship, and the recruiting calendar means you have
to start now to have the offer in hand by spring. So apply either way. Just do not
tell a strategy firm you are leaving, and do not tell yourself you are not.

## Economic consulting, the best fit by a distance

This is the one part of the private sector that uses your actual skills.
Competition economics and regulatory economics run on difference-in-differences,
event studies, merger simulation and damages estimation. The Callaway and
Sant'Anna work on your CV is directly relevant rather than a curiosity, and these
firms hire economics master's graduates as analysts and consultants, many of whom
go on to PhDs afterwards.

The Rhine-Ruhr is unusually good for this. The Bundeskartellamt sits in Bonn, the
competition bar clusters in Düsseldorf, and the firms follow the work.

- **Frontier Economics**, Cologne office. The most obvious single target.
- **Compass Lexecon**, **Charles River Associates**, **NERA**, **RBB Economics**,
  **Oxera**, **E.CA Economics**. Berlin, Düsseldorf and Brussels offices, and
  several recruit into Germany generally rather than one city.
- **DICE Consult**, attached to HHU Düsseldorf, which keeps a foot in the academic
  world.
- **Copenhagen Economics** and **Positive Agenda Consulting** for the policy end.

Search on "competition economics analyst", "economic consultant", and the firm
names directly. Postings are thin and irregular, so speculative applications work
better here than anywhere else on this page.

## Strategy and management consulting

Düsseldorf is the German centre of gravity for this, and Cologne and Frankfurt add
more. Entry pay for a master's graduate runs roughly 60,000 to 90,000 euros
including bonus, which is well above anything on the academic track.

- **McKinsey**, whose German head office is in Düsseldorf. **BCG** and **Bain**,
  Düsseldorf, Cologne and Frankfurt.
- **Simon-Kucher**, headquartered in **Bonn**. Founded by economists, built on
  pricing, and the most economics-heavy of the large consultancies. Thirty minutes
  from Cologne. This should be near the top of your list.
- **Roland Berger**, **Oliver Wyman**, **Kearney**, all with Düsseldorf offices.
- **Deloitte**, **PwC**, **EY**, **KPMG**. Look specifically at economic advisory,
  valuation, transfer pricing and forensic units rather than general audit, since
  those use econometrics and hire economists.

Processes are fast, usually four to eight weeks from application to offer, and
intake is rolling. Applying in September or October means a start around January.

## Finance and insurance

- **Cologne is an insurance city.** AXA Germany, Zurich Gruppe Deutschland,
  Generali Deutschland, Gothaer and DEVK all have their German head offices here.
  Look at risk, pricing, actuarial and data analytics functions. These are
  quantitative jobs that will take an econometrician.
- **Düsseldorf.** HSBC Germany is headquartered here, and **NRW.BANK**, the state
  development bank, is a good public-sector-adjacent option with real economics
  work. Also Provinzial and ERGO.
- **Frankfurt**, about an hour by ICE. Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank, DZ Bank, DWS,
  Union Investment, Deka, and the German offices of the international banks. Note
  the commuting caveats in [`README.md`](README.md), which bite less here because
  the pay is higher.
- **Sparkasse KölnBonn** and the cooperative banks for a local, lower-intensity
  option.

## Corporate economics and analytics

Large employers with head offices in range that run economics, pricing or
analytics functions: **Henkel**, **Metro**, **Vodafone**, **Trivago** (Düsseldorf),
**Bayer** (Leverkusen), **Deutsche Post DHL**, **Deutsche Telekom** (Bonn),
**E.ON**, **Evonik** (Essen), **Lufthansa** (Frankfurt Airport, the shortest
commute on either list).

## Qualification filter, private sector version

The 1,200 euro floor from the research track is irrelevant here, so the filter
changes.

Reject if any of these is true.

- Below 45,000 euros a year for a full-time graduate role
- Outside the Cologne, Düsseldorf and Frankfurt range
- Requires current enrolment, since the M.Sc. finishes in September 2026
- Requires a professional qualification you do not have, such as a completed
  actuarial exam or a CFA
- Requires fluent German at a level beyond yours, which rules out some
  client-facing insurance and public-sector roles but almost no consulting or
  banking ones

## What changes in the application itself

The CV has to change. The academic CV leads with the thesis, the methods and the
proposal, which is right for a chair and wrong for McKinsey. A private-sector CV
leads with results, tools and scale, so the ProUni thesis becomes a line about
building a causal-inference pipeline over five million records in R, not a line
about Callaway and Sant'Anna.

Keep both versions. The academic one is in
[`../cv/academic/`](../cv/academic/). The industry version and the Colliers letter
that produced an interview are in [`../letters/industry/`](../letters/industry/),
which is the right starting point for this track.

The fit paragraph method in [`pipeline.md`](pipeline.md) still applies. What
changes is what it points at, a client problem or a business question rather than
a research agenda.
