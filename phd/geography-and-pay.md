# PhD targets by country, ranked by pay

Your criteria: Germany's neighbours, plus Spain and Italy, preferably well paid.
That turns out to be a useful filter, because Germany borders three of the four
best-paid PhD systems in Europe.

_Last updated: September 2026. Figures are gross and approximate. Always check
the actual vacancy, since national scales move every year._

## The short answer

Denmark, Switzerland and the Netherlands pay two to three times what Spain and
Italy pay for the same work. All three are neighbours, all three run PhDs as
salaried employment rather than as a student stipend, and all three are strong in
applied micro. That is where the effort should go.

Spain and Italy stay on the list because you asked for them and because CEMFI,
the Barcelona School of Economics and the EUI are genuinely good places. Just go
in knowing the pay is roughly half.

## Tier 1: go here first

### Denmark

**University of Copenhagen, CEBI.** This is the single best target on the list.
The Center for Economic Behavior and Inequality works on exactly your question,
inequality and the mechanisms behind it, with register data of the kind you
already know how to handle. Danish administrative registers are the closest thing
in Europe to the RAIS data you used in the thesis, which makes your method
directly transferable rather than merely adjacent.

**Sonja Settele is at Copenhagen.** She already suggested Barcelona to you, which
means the door is open. Ask her about Copenhagen too. A warm contact at the
best-paid, best-fitting department is worth more than ten cold emails, and it
costs one message.

Also worth watching: Aarhus (strong econometrics), and the Copenhagen Business
School.

Danish PhD positions are advertised as jobs with deadlines through the year
rather than as one annual admissions round, so check the vacancy pages regularly
instead of waiting for a cycle.

### Switzerland

Highest pay anywhere, and the route is a direct application to a chair rather
than a centralised admissions committee, which suits you. You have no letters yet
and no RA experience, and a professor who wants you can hire you regardless.

Targets: **University of Zurich** (very strong in education and labour economics,
and in the beliefs and perceptions literature your proposal builds on), **ETH
Zurich**, **University of Bern**, **University of Basel**, **University of
Lausanne** and **HEC Lausanne**, **University of St. Gallen**, **Geneva**.

Zurich and Lausanne also run structured doctoral programmes with formal deadlines
in the winter, so do both, the programme application and the direct email.

### Netherlands

Salaried, well paid, and you have a personal hook. You spent an Erasmus semester
at **RSM Erasmus University in Rotterdam**, which makes Erasmus School of
Economics the natural first email rather than a cold one.

Targets: **Tinbergen Institute** (Amsterdam and Rotterdam, the standard research
master then PhD route), **Erasmus School of Economics**, **CentER at Tilburg**,
**VU Amsterdam**, **University of Amsterdam**, and **Maastricht**, which is about
90 minutes from Düsseldorf and therefore the only foreign option you could take
without moving.

Dutch positions are posted individually on AcademicTransfer as jobs. The template
is in [`international/email-netherlands.md`](international/email-netherlands.md).

## Tier 2: solid, apply if the fit is there

### Austria

**University of Vienna**, **WU Vienna**, **IHS Vienna**, **University of
Innsbruck**, **JKU Linz**. Pay is comparable to Germany once the 14 monthly
payments are counted. German-speaking, so no language barrier and no relocation
shock. Vienna has a good applied micro group.

### Belgium

**KU Leuven**, **UCLouvain (IRES)**, **Ghent**, **Antwerp**. The money comes
through FWO in Flanders or FNRS in Wallonia, and those fellowships are
tax-exempt, so the net is better than the gross figure implies. The catch is that
you usually co-apply with a supervisor, which means finding the supervisor first
and applying to the funder second. The FWO round typically closes at the start of
December, so this one has an earlier clock than the German cycle.

Template: [`international/email-belgium.md`](international/email-belgium.md).

### Luxembourg

**University of Luxembourg** and **LISER**, the Luxembourg Institute of
Socio-Economic Research. LISER in particular does labour and inequality work with
administrative data. Small, so few slots, but very well paid and low tax. Worth
one email.

### France

Lower pay, but **PSE**, **TSE** and **CREST** are top-tier departments and your
French (B1, DELF) is an asset rather than a requirement. Treat France as a
quality play, not a pay play. Template:
[`international/email-france.md`](international/email-france.md).

## Tier 3: requested, but understand the trade-off

### Spain

**CEMFI Madrid** is excellent and small, and its master's-then-PhD structure is
the standard route. **Barcelona School of Economics**, **Universitat Pompeu
Fabra**, **UAB** and **Universitat de Barcelona** are the Barcelona cluster Sonja
Settele pointed you to. UPF and BSE are genuinely strong in applied micro.

Two things to know. First, the pay is roughly half the Dutch figure. Second, the
BSE and UPF route generally expects the GRE, which matters for your open GRE
decision, because Bonn wants it too. If you sit the GRE for Bonn, Barcelona comes
free. If you do not, most of Spain closes.

Deadlines cluster in January and February for autumn starts.

### Italy

**European University Institute, Florence.** The best Italian option by a
distance. It is an international institution rather than a normal Italian
university, it is fully funded by national grants, and the economics department
is strong. The deadline is usually **31 January**. The German-funded grant route
is administered separately, so check which national quota you fall under as a
German-resident applicant with Tunisian nationality, since eligibility follows
funding source rather than location.

**Bocconi Milan** pays at the top of the Italian band and has a good applied micro
group. Public universities pay the statutory minimum, which is low.

## What this means for the plan

The German winter cycle stays the core, because the deadlines are fixed and the
applications are already half-written. The international layer sits on top of it,
and it splits cleanly into two kinds of work.

The **cold-email countries**, Switzerland, Denmark, Belgium and Luxembourg, run on
supervisor contact rather than deadlines. You can start those this week and they
cost one email each. There is no reason to wait.

The **deadline countries**, the Netherlands, Spain, Italy and France, need a
calendar entry and a document set. January and February is when they land, which
is the same window as Bonn and DIW, so plan for one heavy month rather than a
steady drip.
