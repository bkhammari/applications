## On the Tunisia connection, and how not to use it

The personal motivation is real. The framing needs care.

**The CRC works in Kenya, Tanzania, Namibia and Zambia**, along the Kenyan Rift
Valley and LAPSSET corridor, the Southern Agricultural Growth Corridor of Tanzania,
and the Kavango-Zambezi conservation area. That is East and Southern Africa. It is
not the Maghreb, and North Africa is a different research context entirely.

So leading with "my parents are from Tunisia, I am interested in Africa" would read
to a specialist as someone treating a continent as one place. It would cost you
credibility rather than build it.

What is a genuine hook is already in your thesis. It measures a **rural-urban
divide in how an education policy transmits into wages**, across 558 subnational
regions, and finds that the effect depends on where you are. That is a real
methodological and substantive overlap, it is defensible under questioning, and it
is what both drafts lead with.

If you want the personal motivation in, put it in a second conversation or an
interview, as motivation rather than as expertise.

## Say the limitation yourself

Both drafts state plainly that the empirical work is on Brazil rather than Africa,
and that what you bring is method and spatial design rather than field knowledge.

A specialist spots that gap in ten seconds. Naming it first turns it from something
they catch into something you have already thought about.

I am finishing my M.Sc. in Economics at the University of Cologne this month and I am looking for a doctoral position from October 2026. I am writing about C08 Job Futures specifically.

My question is direct: with the third funding phase starting, do you expect to recruit a doctoral researcher for the project?

I ask because my thesis did a smaller version of what C08 does. Graded 1.0, it estimated the causal effect of a national higher education policy on formal-sector wages across 558 subnational regions, using the Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) heterogeneity-robust staggered difference-in-differences estimator on a panel of roughly five million linked employer-employee records that I built in R. Two results are relevant here. The effects differ sharply between rural and urban labour markets, and conventional two-way fixed effects estimation reverses their sign. Your project asks what different types of jobs do to income, inequality and gender roles using household, individual and employer data, which is a version of the same question with better data and a harder identification problem.

I should be straightforward about one limitation. My empirical work is on Brazil, not on East or Southern Africa, so what I would bring is the method and the regional design rather than field knowledge of your sites.
