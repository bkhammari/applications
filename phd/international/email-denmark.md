# Denmark and the Nordics, cold email to a potential supervisor

**Where to send:** University of Copenhagen, and specifically **CEBI**, the Center
for Economic Behavior and Inequality, which is the closest field match anywhere in
Europe. Also Aarhus University, Copenhagen Business School, and if you widen
beyond Denmark, the Stockholm School of Economics, IIES Stockholm, Uppsala and
IFAU.

**Why here first:** Danish PhDs are full employee positions with pension, at
roughly DKK 35,000 to 40,000 a month, which is the highest floor in Europe. Danish
administrative registers are the European equivalent of the RAIS data you already
worked with, so your method transfers directly rather than needing to be
relearned. Positions are advertised as individual job vacancies through the year
rather than in one annual round, so check the vacancy pages regularly.

**Warm contact:** Sonja Settele is an Associate Professor at Cologne and an
**external member of CEBI**, having been an Assistant Professor there before she
moved. She is not inside the department any more, so the ask is an introduction
rather than a position. You met her in person in July, so this is a follow-up. Use
the version at the bottom for her.

**Adapt:** the `[Professor X]` line and the one-line fit paragraph.

---

Subject: PhD inquiry: inequality and policy evaluation with register data

Dear Professor [X],

My name is Baha Khammari. I am completing my M.Sc. in Economics at the University of Cologne (expected September 2026) and I am looking for a PhD position in applied microeconomics, working on inequality and the labor market.

My master's thesis (grade 1.0) estimated the wage effects of Brazil's ProUni scholarship program using the Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) staggered difference-in-differences estimator on roughly 5 million linked employer-employee observations, and found effects that are strongly heterogeneous by ethnicity and region and that conventional two-way fixed effects estimation masks entirely. My proposed PhD compares which higher education access mechanism, scholarships, loans, or quotas, most reduces labor market inequality, and for whom.

I am drawn to [department or center] because the Danish register data allow exactly the design my thesis was limited by, following individuals rather than repeated cross-sections. [One line on this professor's specific work: a paper, a method, a policy question.]

Would you be open to a short conversation, or could you tell me whether you expect to advertise a PhD position in the coming year? I have attached my CV and a research proposal.
