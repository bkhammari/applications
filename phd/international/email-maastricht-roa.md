## Why ROA rather than the GSBE portal

**ROA is the Research Centre for Education and the Labour Market.** That is the
title of your research agenda, so this is the closest institutional fit anywhere
on your list. Founded 1986, part of the Maastricht School of Business and
Economics, one of the largest institutes in the field. Its overarching theme is
the acquisition and depreciation of human capital across the life course in
relation to labour market dynamics, and it runs roughly thirty doctoral
candidates.

**GSBE expects a supervisor and a funded position to be identified before you
apply.** So writing into the portal cold is the paperwork that follows the
approach rather than the approach itself. Naming preferred supervisors is required
by the central route anyway, which is why the email below asks who the right
person is rather than asking to be admitted.

Review is rolling through May 2027 for the September 2027 intake and Studielink
opens on 1 October 2026, so nothing is lost by writing to people first. The
central address for external candidates, as a fallback or in parallel, is
`gsbe-phd-application@maastrichtuniversity.nl`, which wants a CV and a one-page
motivation letter naming the staff you would want to work with.

If any of them, or a colleague I have missed, is open to a doctoral candidate, I would be grateful for a pointer.

My master's thesis was graded 1.0. It estimated the causal wage effect of Brazil's ProUni scholarship programme using the Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) heterogeneity-robust staggered difference-in-differences estimator, on a panel of roughly five million linked employer-employee records that I built in R. The main result is a non-monotonic dose-response whose sign conventional two-way fixed effects estimation reverses. The doctorate I propose compares scholarships, loans and quotas as access mechanisms, and asks which one reduces labour market inequality, and for whom. It is designed to transfer across country settings, and Dutch register data would allow the individual-level version of the design that Brazilian data did not.

Two practical points. I spent an Erasmus semester at RSM in Rotterdam, so the Netherlands is familiar, and I live in Düsseldorf, which is about ninety minutes from Maastricht.
