_Last updated: 17 September 2026, after checking the Dutch vacancy channel._

## The route that was missing: a Dutch PhD is an advertised job

Every funded PhD position at Maastricht is published as a vacancy, on
**AcademicTransfer** and on the university's own job site, and filled by applying
to it. There is no central programme intake to wait for and no committee cycle.

This is the Dutch version of the same insight as
[`../e13-direct.md`](../e13-direct.md), and Maastricht had been treated here as a
programme-application problem when half of it is a job-application problem.

**It also pays better than anything else on the list.** The most recent Maastricht
economics PhD vacancy advertised **€3,059 to €3,881 gross a month** over four
years, plus 8 percent holiday allowance and an 8.3 percent year-end bonus. German
E13 at 65 percent is 2,700 to 3,000. The UNU-MERIT fellowship below is 1,650 net.

Two places to watch, weekly:

- `academictransfer.com`, filtered to Maastricht University
- `vacancies.maastrichtuniversity.nl`

## MILE runs an annual round, and the theme is his thesis

The **Department of Macro, International and Labour Economics** advertises PhD
positions under a department-wide theme called **Inequality Dynamics**: how
environmental, political and technological transformations affect firms,
individuals and societies differently, and the heterogeneous paths adjustment takes,
with inequality as either a driver or an outcome.

That is a restatement of the master's thesis in a different setting.

It has run at least twice, which is what makes it worth planning around rather than
hoping for.

| Round | Deadline | Start | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 30 April 2025 | 2025 | |
| 2026 | 1 February 2026 | 1 Sep 2026, or by 1 Sep 2027 | €3,059 to €3,881, four years. Supervisor Dr Julian Ashwin. The project paired inequality with health and lifespan |

So **expect a 2027 round between February and April**. The 2026 application asked
for a CV, a motivation letter, a research proposal of at most 500 words,
transcripts and two references. Everything except the 500-word proposal already
exists, and cutting the proposal to 500 words is the same work UNU-MERIT needs at
2 to 3 pages.

**One thing to check rather than assume.** The 2026 posting asked for *"a research
master's degree equivalent to 120 ECTS (MSc, MPhil, or MRes)"*. A German two-year
M.Sc. at 120 ECTS should satisfy that, but *research master* is a specific category
in the Dutch system. It is worth one line to the department before building a plan
on it, and it is a reasonable reason to write to them now.

**Tilburg answers the same question favourably**, which is a reason to expect
Maastricht will too rather than to assume it. CentER asks for *"an academic
Master's or Research Master's degree"* and treats Research Master holders as
preferred rather than required, and it waives the GRE for an EU MSc carrying 24
ECTS of quantitative and research methods. Written up in
[`../../apply/applications/tilburg-university-phd-candidate-in-economics-center-digital/posting.md`](../../apply/applications/tilburg-university-phd-candidate-in-economics-center-digital/posting.md).

## UNU-MERIT, with the dates corrected

The **IEGSD** programme at the UNU institute, run jointly with Maastricht
University. Public policy strand covers population, development and labour
economics, social protection, poverty and inequality, and policy evaluation.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Call opens** | **1 November 2026** |
| **Closes** | **15 February 2027** |
| Selection | March and April 2027 |
| Starts | September 2027 |
| Fellowship | **€1,650 net a month for four years**, up to 10 awarded competitively, tuition waived |
| GRE | Not required. This is the point |
| English | IELTS accepted. His 8.0 clears it |
| Proposal | 2 to 3 pages |
| Apply | `application.unu-merit.nl/phd/form.php` |

Earlier notes here said roughly €1,700 and a 15 February deadline inferred from the
previous year. Both are now confirmed from the programme, and the figure is net
rather than gross.

**Open question.** The number of reference letters could not be confirmed from
UNU-MERIT's own pages, which list the application letter, proposal, CV,
certificates and an English test but do not state a letter count. The earlier note
here said one. Ask `phdprogramme@merit.unu.edu` before relying on it, because one
letter versus two changes whether Settele has to be asked in time.

**The honest catch is still the money.** 1,650 net over four years, against 3,059
to 3,881 gross for an advertised Maastricht position. Take UNU-MERIT as the route
that cannot be closed by a GRE, not as the first choice.

## MILE, the people

Two names worth an E13-style enquiry, using the template in
[`../templates/email-e13-inquiry.md`](../templates/email-e13-inquiry.md). Ask
whether a position is expected, and mention the Inequality Dynamics theme by name.

- **Prof. Bart Golsteyn**, head of MILE. Human capital and social economics.
- **Prof. Lex Borghans**, Professor of Labor Economics and Social Policy. Human
  capital, economics of education, adjustment processes in the labour market. The
  closest single match at Maastricht outside ROA, and long associated with ROA.
- **Dr Julian Ashwin** ran the 2026 Inequality Dynamics vacancy, so he is the person
  who knows whether there is a 2027 one.

**Thomas Dohmen** sits here too, as Professor of Education and the Labour Market.
He was emailed on 8 September and has not replied, so do not write to MILE as
though nobody there has heard from you.

**Department of Microeconomics and Public Economics**, headed by Prof. Kirsten
Rohde, is worth scanning for applied public economists. Rohde's own field is
behavioural and time preferences, so she is a weaker direct fit.

## Addresses: there are two patterns, not one

The earlier note here said Maastricht follows `initial.lastname@`. That was inferred
from one address and it is wrong. Both forms are in use.

| Form | Examples |
|---|---|
| `initial.lastname@` | `t.huijts@`, `b.golsteyn@` |
| `firstname.lastname@` | `julian.ashwin@`, `lex.borghans@` |

All at `maastrichtuniversity.nl`. Only `t.huijts@` and `julian.ashwin@` came from a
primary source. **Verify on the staff page before sending**, and note that the UM
staff pages render in JavaScript, so a plain fetch returns nothing and the page has
to be opened in a browser. `cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl` confirms affiliations but
not addresses.

Guessing `l.borghans@` from the old rule would have bounced.
