# Maastricht, beyond ROA

ROA was the first approach and it produced a useful answer: no funded openings now,
but Tim Huijts is circulating the message to Cörvers, Haelermans and Fouarge. That
is one door, held ajar. Maastricht has three more.

_Last updated: 10 September 2026._

## UNU-MERIT is the find

The **United Nations University Maastricht Economic and Social Research Institute**
runs its own doctoral programme, **IEGSD** (Innovation, Economics, Governance and
Sustainable Development), jointly with Maastricht University. About 100 doctoral
candidates, and up to **10 funded fellowships** awarded competitively each round.

Its public policy and governance strand covers governance and institutions,
migration and development, **population, development and labour economics**,
**social protection**, **poverty and inequality**, and **policy analysis and
evaluation**. That is the subject of the thesis, in a development setting.

**Every requirement lines up, and one of them matters a lot.**

| Requirement | Status |
|---|---|
| **No GRE** | Not mentioned anywhere in the requirements. This is the point |
| Master's in economics or a related field | Met in September 2026 |
| English fluency, IELTS 7.0 minimum | IELTS 8.0, clears it |
| Research proposal, 2 to 3 pages | Already written, needs cutting to length |
| **One** letter of recommendation | Only one, and Pinger and Anna Person agreed on 9 September |
| Motivation letter, CV, transcripts, passport | All exist |

**Deadline: 15 February.** The last round closed 15 February 2026, so expect the same
date in 2027. Starts in September. Apply at `application.unu-merit.nl/phd/form.php`.

**The honest catch is the money.** The fellowship pays roughly **€1,700 a month**
plus a €3,500 research budget and tuition, over four years. That is well below a
Dutch employee PhD at €2,900 and up, and below German E13. The programme is
described as mainly grant or scholarship based, and applicants are encouraged to
secure external funding, which means not everyone admitted is funded.

So it is a strong fit on subject and on requirements, and a weak one on pay. Given
that the GRE decision closes Bonn, UPF and the Barcelona School of Economics, a
funded route that does not ask for the GRE is worth a lot.

## MILE, the labour economics department

**Department of Macro, International and Labour Economics.** Two names worth an
E13-style enquiry, using the template in
[`../templates/email-e13-inquiry.md`](../templates/email-e13-inquiry.md).

- **Prof. Bart Golsteyn**, head of MILE. Human capital and social economics.
- **Prof. Lex Borghans**, Professor of Labor Economics and Social Policy. Human
  capital, labour economics, economics of education. The closest single match at
  Maastricht outside ROA, and he is also long associated with ROA.

Note that **Thomas Dohmen** sits here too, as Professor of Education and the Labour
Market. He was already emailed on 8 September, so do not write to MILE as though
nobody there has heard from you. If Golsteyn or Borghans replies, mention it.

**Department of Microeconomics and Public Economics**, headed by Prof. Kirsten
Rohde, has about 20 faculty. Rohde's own field is behavioural and time preferences,
so she is a weaker direct fit, but the department is worth scanning for applied
public economists.
