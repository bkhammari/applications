# Spain and Italy, application notes and inquiry email

These two work differently from the cold-email countries. Spain and Italy admit
through structured programmes with fixed deadlines, so the application form is the
main route and the email is a supporting move rather than the decisive one.

**Where to apply.** Spain: CEMFI Madrid, Barcelona School of Economics,
Universitat Pompeu Fabra, Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona, Universitat de
Barcelona. Italy: the European University Institute in Florence, then Bocconi
Milan.

**The pay trade-off, stated plainly.** Spanish predoc contracts run roughly €1,300
to €1,600 a month and Italian doctoral grants roughly €1,350 to €1,800, against
€2,500 to €3,250 in the Netherlands and more in Switzerland and Denmark. CEMFI,
the BSE, the EUI and Bocconi add their own funding and sit at the top of those
bands, but the gap does not close. Apply here for the department, not for the
money.

**Two things that decide whether Spain is open.**

The GRE. UPF and the BSE generally expect it, and so does Bonn. One test covers
all three. Skip it and most of Spain closes along with Bonn, which makes this a
single decision rather than three.

The deadlines. Both countries cluster in January and February for autumn starts,
the same window as Bonn and DIW. The EUI deadline is usually **31 January**. Plan
for one heavy month.

**On the EUI specifically.** It is an international institution rather than a
normal Italian university, funding comes through national grants, and eligibility
follows the funding source rather than where you live. Check early which national
quota applies to you as a German-resident applicant with Tunisian nationality,
because that determines both eligibility and the size of the grant, and it is not
something to discover in January.

**Sonja Settele suggested Barcelona.** That is a warm lead worth using before the
deadline rather than after. She is at Copenhagen now, so ask her about both in one
message (see [`email-denmark.md`](email-denmark.md)).

## Inquiry email to a potential supervisor

Use this before the formal application, to find out whether anyone in the group
would want to work with you. Adapt the `[Professor X]` line and the fit paragraph.

Subject: PhD applicant, applied microeconomics and policy evaluation

Dear Professor [X],

My name is Baha Khammari. I am completing my M.Sc. in Economics at the University of Cologne (expected September 2026), and I intend to apply to the [programme name] doctoral programme for the coming intake.

My master's thesis (grade 1.0) estimated the wage effects of Brazil's ProUni scholarship program using the Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) staggered difference-in-differences estimator on roughly 5 million linked employer-employee observations, finding effects that are strongly heterogeneous by ethnicity and region and that two-way fixed effects estimation masks. The PhD project I propose compares which higher education access mechanism, scholarships, loans, or quotas, most reduces labor market inequality, and for whom.

[One line on why this group specifically: a paper, a method, a data source, a policy question.]

Before I submit the formal application, I wanted to ask whether this direction fits the work going on in your group, and whether you would be open to supervising in this area. I have attached my CV and a research proposal, and I am glad to send the thesis itself.

Best regards,
Baha Khammari
M.Sc. Economics (expected Sep. 2026), University of Cologne
