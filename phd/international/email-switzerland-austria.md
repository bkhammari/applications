# Switzerland and Austria, cold email to a potential supervisor

**Where to send.** Switzerland: University of Zurich (strong in education, labour,
and the beliefs and perceptions literature the proposal builds on), ETH Zurich,
University of Bern, University of Basel, University of Lausanne and HEC Lausanne,
University of St. Gallen, University of Geneva. Austria: University of Vienna, WU
Vienna, IHS Vienna, University of Innsbruck, JKU Linz.

**Why this route works for you.** Swiss and Austrian doctoral positions are
usually filled by a chair hiring an assistant directly, not by a central
admissions committee. That matters, because you have no reference letters
confirmed and no research-assistant experience, and neither is needed to start a
conversation with a professor who has a vacancy. Swiss pay is the highest in
Europe, roughly CHF 4,000 to 5,800 a month. Austrian prae-doc posts pay around
€2,600 to €2,900 at 75%, paid 14 times a year.

**Do both routes in Switzerland.** Zurich and Lausanne also run structured
doctoral programmes with winter deadlines. Send the email and file the programme
application. They are separate processes and neither blocks the other.

**Language.** Everything in economics runs in English, so write in English. For
Austria and German-speaking Switzerland you can add one line noting that German
is a working language for you, which helps for teaching duties.

**Adapt:** the `[Professor X]` line and the one-line fit paragraph.

Subject: PhD inquiry: applied microeconomics, education and labor

Dear Professor [X],

My name is Baha Khammari. I am completing my M.Sc. in Economics at the University of Cologne (expected September 2026) and I am looking for a doctoral position in applied microeconomics.

The effects turned out to be strongly heterogeneous by ethnicity and region, and conventional two-way fixed effects estimation hides them. My proposed PhD compares which higher education access mechanism, scholarships, loans, or quotas, most reduces labor market inequality, and for whom, in a design built to transfer across country settings.
