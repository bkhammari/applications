I am applying for the doctoral position on auction-based support schemes for renewable energy in Europe. This statement sets out the training I would bring to it and the training I would have to acquire.

\sectionrule{Background in energy}

My energy training is at Cologne, where the specialisation is taught by the Institute of Energy Economics. I took \textit{Energy Markets and Regulation} with Jun.-Prof.\ Dr.\ Oliver Ruhnau, whose chair is in energy market design, and finished it with a grade of 1.3 on a scale where 1.0 is the maximum. The course covered electricity market design, network regulation, and the instruments used to bring renewable generation into a market that was not built for intermittent supply. I then took \textit{Growth, Energy and Climate Change} with PD Dr.\ Dietmar Lindenberger.

My specialisation seminar paper is on the EU Emissions Trading System. The question it left me with is the one this project also turns on. A support instrument is a set of rules, the rules determine who bids and on what terms, and the aggregate quantity procured follows from that design. An auction for renewable capacity and a cap-and-trade scheme are both attempts to buy an environmental good through a market, and the design detail determines what each one buys.

\sectionrule{Proposed contribution}

Of the four directions in the call, the two I would want to take up are pre-qualification requirements together with completion bonds, and local content requirements. My reason is specific to the identification problem they present.

Auction design in Europe is not a single policy. Member states adopted auctions at different dates and subsequently revised the rules at different dates again: penalty levels, bid bond requirements, realisation deadlines, technology-specific against technology-neutral lots, and local content conditions have each been introduced and amended on separate national timetables. This is staggered adoption of a treatment whose intensity varies across adopters, and it is the setting in which conventional panel estimators fail in a way that is easy to miss.

My master's thesis was built for exactly that setting. It estimated the labour-market effects of a national scholarship programme across 558 regions over the period 2005 to 2019, using the heterogeneity-robust staggered difference-in-differences estimator of Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) with doubly-robust inference, on a panel of roughly five million linked employer-employee records that I constructed. The doubly-robust estimate is a wage effect of $+3.04$ per cent in low-exposure regions. The two-way fixed effects estimate on the same panel is $-0.65$ per cent. The sign reverses because two-way fixed effects draws comparisons between later-treated and already-treated units, and those comparisons are invalid once effects vary across adoption cohorts and over time, as Goodman-Bacon (2021) shows. The thesis was graded 1.0.

The design question I would want to answer at CORE follows from this. Auction theory delivers predictions about how pre-qualification and penalty regimes should change bidding behaviour and completion rates. Testing those predictions against the European record requires an estimator that survives staggered adoption with heterogeneous treatment effects, and the diagnostics that establish whether it has survived. That is the part of the project I could do on arrival.

The call asks for graduate training in microeconomic theory, industrial organization and game theory. My graduate coursework is econometric. My strongest marks are in econometrics, mathematics for economists and energy regulation, and my microeconomics grade is 3.0, which is average on the German scale. I would begin the theoretical component of this project from a lower base than a candidate trained in industrial organization, and the transcript accompanying this application shows that.

Two things qualify it. Mas-Colell, Whinston and Green and Osborne and Rubinstein are the standard the call names, and reaching that standard is a matter of coursework and reading that a first year is designed to accommodate. The empirical craft is the harder thing to add later, and I have it already.
