## Verdict: no, but it is the closest of the three

**This one does not fail on eligibility.** Economics is explicitly on the list:

> hold an MSc, MA or MPhil degree in **business or economics**, or from related
> fields such as psychology, sociology, engineering, or mathematics

And the RSM connection is real, an Erasmus+ semester there in autumn 2024. So the
thirty-second test in [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) passes on faculty and on
degree. It fails on the other two things.

**The GMAT or GRE.** They want a score *"at or above the 85th percentile"*, minimum
650 GMAT for 2026, with a carve-out to apply without one given *"additional evidence
of excellence"*. That is not a hard block, and the 1.0 thesis and the Dean's List
are real evidence. But it means competing without a number in a pool where others
have one, for a place at a school that asks for it. The no-GRE decision stands and
this does not overturn it.

**The subject.** Neurodivergent entrepreneurs, at a business school, in a strategy
and entrepreneurship department. Nothing in the toolkit applies. Staggered
difference-in-differences on five million linked employment records is not what this
project needs, and a four-year doctorate in entrepreneurship research is a change of
field rather than a job.
