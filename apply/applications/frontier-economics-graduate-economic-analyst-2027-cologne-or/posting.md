## Why this rather than the Amsterdam posting

The Amsterdam Consultant role (JR10138) was rejected on two hard requirements.
"Fluent level Dutch (C2 level or equivalent) is required", justified in the posting
by Dutch-speaking clients and Dutch-language deliverables, and "expertise in
Competition Economics, with several years of experience at an economics consultancy
or regulator". Consultant is not an entry grade at Frontier.

Frontier had **four** open roles worldwide on 8 September 2026, and all four were
out of reach:

| Role | Location | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| Consultant, Competition | Amsterdam | C2 Dutch, several years' experience |
| Senior Consultant, Competition | Brussels | Well above entry |
| Principal, Competition | Brussels | Well above entry |
| Graduate Economic Analyst, PhD/Experienced track | Amsterdam | Needs Dutch and a PhD, and the deadline was 31 Jan 2026 |

Four openings globally is the whole argument for speculative applications in
economic consulting. The postings are thin and irregular, so waiting for the right
one to appear is a losing strategy.

## The route that does work

The Graduate Economic Analyst programme is the correct entry point, and the
requirements fit almost exactly.

- "Predicted or achieved 2:1 or above in an Economics-focused degree (**or
  equivalent: Germany 2.0 or better**), plus a Master's with a strong economic
  focus". Met.
- "Micro-economics knowledge and experience". Met.
- "**Advanced Excel skills and proficiency with R or Stata**". Met, and this is the
  strongest single line. R for the thesis, advanced Excel from PwC.
- "Fluent in the language of the office you are applying to (except Brussels, where
  English is sufficient)". This is what kills Amsterdam and what makes **Cologne
  and Berlin work**, since German is native. Brussels also works on English alone.
- Analyst positions "typically start in September/October", which lines up with
  finishing the M.Sc. in September 2026.

Note the two tracks. The PhD/Experienced track enters in the second year. Without a
PhD the standard graduate track is the right one.

Frontier has 350+ economists across Amsterdam, Berlin, Brussels, **Cologne**,
Dublin, London, Madrid, Paris and Prague, and is entirely employee-owned.

## What they actually asked for

1. R or Stata proficiency plus advanced Excel
2. Micro-economics, applied to competition cases: merger control, market studies,
   antitrust, digital regulation, litigation and damages
3. German for the Cologne and Berlin offices, English for Brussels
4. Communication, specifically turning technical analysis into client output
