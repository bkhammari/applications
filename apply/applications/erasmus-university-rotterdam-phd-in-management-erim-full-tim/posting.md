## Which Erasmus faculty, and why it has to be this one

Erasmus has two doctoral routes and only one of them is open to him.

| Route | Test requirement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **ESE**, Erasmus School of Economics | **GRE required of every entering PhD student**, and applicants scoring below **160 on the quantitative section will not be considered** | **Closed.** The no-GRE decision holds, and the GMAT he offered to sit is not accepted here |
| **ERIM / RSM**, PhD in Management | GMAT or GRE ideally at the 85th percentile, **but applications are accepted without a score** given other evidence of excellence, and ERIM names **top 15 per cent grades** as an example | **Open, and no test needed.** He is top 5 per cent with a Dean's List and a 1,0 thesis |

That is the whole decision. The economics faculty is shut by a rule he has already
decided against. The management school is open, wants no test from him, and is the
part of Erasmus he actually attended.

**This also means the GMAT is probably wasted money.** He offered to sit it. Unless
he wants the extra signal for its own sake, top 5 per cent already does the job
ERIM asks the test to do.

## What is prepared

**`research-statement.pdf`, two pages.** ERIM caps it at two pages, 11-point,
single-spaced, and this hits all three.

The argument it makes, because it is worth knowing before the interview:

His thesis used **RAIS, the Brazilian linked employer--employee register**, and
aggregated five million worker-firm records up to 558 regions. **The firm layer was
in the data and went unused.** The statement proposes going back down to it: which
firms absorb a graduate supply shock and which do not, whether widened access
produces promotion or only entry, and where the gender asymmetry in his own results
comes from, given that majority-female scholarship uptake produced male-skewed
gains.

That is the honest bridge from economics to a management school. It is the same
data and the same methods asking an organisational question, rather than a
pretence of being a management scholar already. The statement says outright that
the organisational theory is something he would be learning rather than arriving
with.

The Rotterdam paragraph is real rather than decorative. He was there on Erasmus+ in
autumn 2024 and calls it the best period of his studies.
