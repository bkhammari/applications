# AD5 reasoning practice

Practice material for the EPSO AD5 (EPSO/AD/427/26) reasoning stage. See
`../epso-ad5.md` for the full competition tracker.

## Format and where the points are

- **Verbal reasoning: 20 questions, 35 minutes. Ranked.** This score decides who
  advances, so it is the priority.
- **Numerical and abstract reasoning: 20 questions combined, 30 minutes.
  Pass/fail.** You need 10 out of 20 to progress, and it does not count toward the
  ranking. Numerical is your relative strength, so the task on abstract is to
  reach a safe margin, not to perfect it.

Pace is about 105 seconds per verbal question and 90 seconds per
numerical/abstract question, so practice against the clock.

## Files

- `verbal-reasoning.md`, a full 20-question verbal set with an answer key and
  explanations. Do it first and timed, since verbal is the ranked block.
- `numerical-reasoning.md`, a 14-question numerical set with worked solutions.
- `abstract-trainer.html`, an interactive generator. Open it in a browser. It
  draws figural sequences, you pick the next figure from five options, and it
  scores you and explains the rule. Endless questions, filterable by rule type
  and by one-rule or two-rule difficulty. Keys 1 to 5 pick an option and Enter
  or the right arrow loads the next, and your running score persists between
  sessions in the browser.

## The abstract-reasoning method

Every figural sequence changes by hidden rules. The reliable method is to isolate
one attribute at a time and check it across all panels before moving to the next.
The attribute types the trainer uses, and the ones EPSO leans on, are:

1. Rotation. A shape turns by a fixed angle each step, often 45 or 90 degrees.
2. Count. The number of elements grows or shrinks by a constant.
3. Shading. A fill flips between states on a fixed cycle.
4. Movement. An element moves position by a fixed step, often around the frame.
5. Size. A shape grows or shrinks by one step each figure.

Most misses happen on items where two rules run at once. Scan rotation across all
panels first, then count, then shading, then movement. Name each rule before you
look at the options. If two options survive, the difference is a single attribute
you have not yet checked. Every item carries equal weight, so guess and move on
rather than overspending.

## Suggested cadence

Do one timed verbal set and one abstract-trainer session (about 20 questions)
several times a week from now to the test window. Track accuracy by type. Spend
the largest share on verbal, then on abstract two-rule items, then a short
numerical warm-up for speed.
