# Mock verbal reasoning (AD5)

Ranked block. Twenty questions is a full test. Here are ten to start, on EU and
economic-policy themes, so you build EU knowledge at the same time. Read each
passage, then pick the one option that follows from the passage alone. Do not use
outside knowledge, even if an option is true in the real world. Target about 105
seconds per question. Answers and explanations are at the end.

---

**1.** The European Central Bank sets monetary policy for the euro area. Its primary objective is price stability, which it defines as inflation of 2% over the medium term. When inflation rises above this target, the ECB may raise its key interest rates to reduce demand. Higher rates increase the cost of borrowing for households and firms. The ECB is independent of national governments.

Which statement follows from the passage?
- A. The ECB's primary objective is to maximise employment.
- B. Raising key interest rates increases the cost of borrowing.
- C. National governments direct the ECB's interest-rate decisions.
- D. The ECB targets 2% inflation over the short term.

**2.** Under the ordinary legislative procedure, the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union act as co-legislators. A proposal is put forward by the European Commission, which holds the right of initiative. Both the Parliament and the Council must agree on an identical text for it to become law. If they cannot agree after two readings, a Conciliation Committee is convened.

Which can be concluded?
- A. The Commission adopts legislation jointly with the Parliament.
- B. A proposal becomes law once the Parliament approves it alone.
- C. The Commission has the right to propose legislation.
- D. The Conciliation Committee meets before the first reading.

**3.** The European single market guarantees the free movement of goods, services, capital, and people. A product lawfully sold in one member state can, in principle, be sold in any other under the principle of mutual recognition. Member states may restrict this freedom on grounds such as public health or safety, provided the restriction is proportionate.

Which statement follows?
- A. Member states can never restrict the free movement of goods.
- B. A product legal in one member state may generally be sold in others.
- C. The single market covers goods but not services.
- D. Restrictions on free movement need not be proportionate.

**4.** Most decisions in the Council of the European Union are taken by qualified majority. A qualified majority is reached when two conditions are met: 55% of member states vote in favour, and those states represent at least 65% of the EU population. A blocking minority must include at least four member states.

Which is correct?
- A. A qualified majority requires unanimity among member states.
- B. Population size is irrelevant to a qualified majority.
- C. A qualified majority requires at least 55% of member states in favour.
- D. Three member states can always form a blocking minority.

**5.** An evaluation of an active labour-market programme found that participants were more likely to be employed one year later than similar non-participants. The evaluation used a difference-in-differences design, comparing changes in employment for participants against a comparison group. The authors caution that the estimate may be biased if participants differed systematically from the comparison group in ways that also affected employment.

Which statement follows?
- A. The programme raised employment with certainty.
- B. The evaluation compared participants only to themselves over time.
- C. The estimate could be biased by systematic differences between the groups.
- D. Difference-in-differences designs are free of bias.

**6.** The Court of Justice of the European Union ensures that EU law is applied uniformly across member states. National courts may, and sometimes must, refer questions on the interpretation of EU law to the Court through the preliminary-ruling procedure. The Court's rulings bind the referring court and set a precedent for similar cases. The Court does not decide the facts of the national case.

Which can be concluded?
- A. The Court decides the facts of national cases.
- B. National courts may refer questions of EU-law interpretation to the Court.
- C. The Court's rulings bind only the parties and set no precedent.
- D. Preliminary rulings are optional in all circumstances.

**7.** The EU's multiannual financial framework (MFF) sets maximum annual spending for a seven-year period. It is adopted by the Council, acting unanimously, after the consent of the European Parliament. The annual budget must remain within the ceilings set by the MFF. The largest shares of EU spending go to cohesion policy and to agriculture.

Which is correct?
- A. The MFF is adopted by the Council by qualified majority.
- B. The annual budget may exceed the MFF ceilings.
- C. The MFF covers a seven-year period.
- D. The Parliament adopts the MFF without the Council.

**8.** Inflation erodes the purchasing power of money. If wages do not rise as fast as prices, real incomes fall even when nominal incomes are unchanged or rising. Central banks may respond to high inflation by tightening monetary policy. Such tightening can slow economic activity and, in the short run, raise unemployment.

Which statement follows?
- A. Real incomes rise whenever nominal incomes rise.
- B. If wages lag prices, real incomes can fall despite rising nominal incomes.
- C. Monetary tightening reliably reduces unemployment.
- D. Inflation increases the purchasing power of money.

**9.** The Schengen Area allows people to cross internal borders without systematic checks. Not every EU member state is part of Schengen, and some non-EU countries participate. In exceptional circumstances, a Schengen state may temporarily reintroduce border controls, for example in response to a serious threat to public policy or internal security.

Which can be concluded?
- A. Every EU member state is part of the Schengen Area.
- B. Only EU member states can participate in Schengen.
- C. A Schengen state may temporarily reintroduce border controls in exceptional cases.
- D. Border controls within Schengen can never be reintroduced.

**10.** A country introduces a subsidised student-loan scheme to widen access to higher education. Enrolment rises in the following years. An analyst notes that enrolment was already trending upward before the scheme, and that a separate scholarship programme expanded at the same time. She concludes that the rise in enrolment cannot be attributed to the loan scheme alone.

Which statement follows?
- A. The loan scheme was the sole cause of rising enrolment.
- B. Enrolment fell after the loan scheme was introduced.
- C. Other factors make it hard to attribute the enrolment rise to the loan scheme alone.
- D. The scholarship programme had no effect on enrolment.

---

## Answer key

1. **B.** The passage states higher rates increase the cost of borrowing. A is wrong (the objective is price stability), C is wrong (the ECB is independent), D is wrong (medium term, not short).
2. **C.** The Commission holds the right of initiative. A and B misstate who legislates, and the Conciliation Committee comes after two readings, not before.
3. **B.** Mutual recognition means a product lawful in one state may generally be sold in others. A and D contradict the proportionate-restriction sentence, and C is wrong because services are included.
4. **C.** The 55% condition is stated directly. A is wrong (qualified majority, not unanimity), B ignores the 65% population condition, and a blocking minority needs at least four states, not three.
5. **C.** The authors explicitly caution about bias from systematic differences. A overstates certainty, B misdescribes the design, and D is a general claim the passage does not make.
6. **B.** National courts may refer interpretation questions. A contradicts the last sentence, C contradicts the precedent sentence, and D ignores "sometimes must."
7. **C.** The seven-year period is stated. A is wrong (unanimously), B contradicts "must remain within," and D omits the Council.
8. **B.** This restates the real-income point. A ignores the wage-price gap, C reverses the unemployment effect, and D reverses the purchasing-power effect.
9. **C.** The temporary reintroduction of controls is stated. A and B contradict "not every EU state" and "some non-EU countries," and D contradicts the exception.
10. **C.** The analyst points to a pre-existing trend and a concurrent programme. A and D overstate, and B contradicts "enrolment rises."

Score out of 10. On the real test this block is ranked, so treat anything below 8 as a signal to keep drilling the "supported by the passage alone" habit.
