My name is Baha Khammari. I am completing my M.Sc. in Economics in your department at the University of Cologne (expected September 2026) and am looking for a PhD position in applied microeconomics. Prof. Michael Krause suggested I contact you directly.

My research evaluates how public policies shape labor market inequality, using heterogeneity-robust difference-in-differences methods on administrative data. My master's thesis (grade 1.0) estimated the causal effect of Brazil's ProUni scholarship program on formal-sector wages, applying the Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) staggered DiD estimator to roughly 5 million RAIS observations across 558 microregions. The main finding, a non-monotonic dose-response that conventional TWFE estimation reverses in sign, sits close to your methodological work on event-study designs and your substantive interest in the distributional effects of public policy.

My proposed PhD compares which higher education access mechanism, scholarships, loans, or quotas, most reduces labor market inequality, and for whom. I use Brazil as the empirical setting but design the questions and methods to transfer to other contexts, including Germany. Your work on policy evaluation and inequality, together with the ECONtribute environment, would be an excellent fit.
