My research asks which higher education access mechanism — scholarships, loans, or quotas — is most effective at reducing labor market inequality, and for whom. I use Brazil as an empirical setting because it implemented all three at national scale, but the questions and methods are designed to transfer to other contexts, including Germany and the EU.

Your work on perceived returns to education addresses the demand side of the same question my project studies from the supply side: you show that beliefs about returns drive enrollment decisions; I estimate the actual causal returns by mechanism and demographic group. My master's thesis (grade: 1.0), which applied the Callaway & Sant'Anna (2021) staggered DiD estimator to ~5 million administrative observations, found that conventional TWFE estimation masks significant heterogeneity by ethnicity and region. The gender asymmetry I document — persistent wage gaps despite majority-female scholarship uptake — also connects to your ERC project on gender inequality in labor markets.
